# You can keep an idea without starting another project

While working, you think of something else you could try. Starting every possibility would leave too many things in progress. Leaving it unwritten makes it easy to lose.

This folder holds possibilities for later consideration. **Writing an idea down does not commit you to doing it.** It gives you a chance to decide again when the time is right.

## Record the problem behind the idea

“Change how the reading group works” leaves you guessing later. Add what you wanted to improve:

> When we only summarize the book, we rarely talk about anything that changed in daily life.
>
> What if everyone brought one action they tried after reading to the next meeting?

Now the idea has a purpose. Add anything you need to check and a small way to try it. If you do act on it, you will have a useful starting point.

## How does this differ from a fleeting note?

Use [[_Zettelkasten-FleetingNotes.en]] to catch a thought as it arrives. During review, bring possibilities you want to keep developing here.

That is one way to use the two folders together, rather than a requirement that every idea pass through Fleeting Notes. The useful part is returning to what you captured and deciding what deserves attention.

When you have an outcome and a plan, connect the idea to [[_Projects.en]]. If you decide against it, keep the reason if it may matter later. If your thinking becomes an independent explanation, develop it in [[_Zettelkasten-PermanentNotes.en]].

When reviewing an idea, ask whether it matters now and what the smallest useful attempt would be.
