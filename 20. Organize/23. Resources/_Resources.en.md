# Find what you already learned

You need something you studied before, but cannot find the explanation you liked. You start another search and read similar material again. *Resources* gives that earlier learning a place you can return to.

Keep more than a list of things to read. **Preserve what you understood, where it came from, and possible ways to use it.**

## Put it on a shelf you can take it down from

A note about reading methods may belong under personal development or another relevant subject. When you begin a reading experiment and will work on that material, bring it into the project folder. Shared references can stay here and be linked from several projects.

At the end of the project, return improved materials and new reusable knowledge here. Resources supplies project materials and receives what the project has helped you develop.

A recognizable title and a useful *Distill* summary help you decide quickly whether you found the right note. Keep the details and source in its body.

**Reference is a note type; Resources is a place.** Moving a reference note here does not remove its type or source.

## Use classification and connections together

A folder gives related material a home you can browse. Links let the same note support several questions and projects without keeping duplicate copies.

If you need a reading route through a subject, make an index note with links and short explanations. Folders show where material lives, an index suggests a way through it, and links explain relationships. [[_Second-Brain-Design.en]] places these roles in the wider system.

## What the numbers mean

10, 20, and 30 mark the broad flow of the vault. Numbers such as 100 and 200 inside Resources identify subjects. They are not priorities or a study sequence, and gaps do not need filling.

| Example shelf | Possible contents |
|---|---|
| 📖100. Faith | Religious study and references |
| 📖200. Work and Business | Work practices, business models, industry research |
| 📖300. Economics | Economic concepts and study material |
| 📖400. Personal Development | Learning methods, development skills, exercise knowledge |
| 📖500. Humanities and Knowledge | Humanities, knowledge management, reading records |
| 📖600. Politics and Diplomacy (North Korea) | References on these subjects |
| 📖900. Relationships | Communication and interpersonal learning |
| 📖1000. Reference | Periodic notes and vault support materials |

📖 marks a broad group; 📚 marks a subgroup, such as `📚501. Books`. Start with the subjects you need. You do not have to memorize the numbering system.

## Add another route when the collection grows

Create subfolders when a subject becomes hard to browse. If a question spans several folders, gather links and short explanations in an index, often called a *Map of Content* or *MOC*.

For material that crosses subjects, choose one natural home and link to it from elsewhere. Imagine when you would look for it.

Change the subjects to fit your interests. If you move Books, Periodic Notes, Templates, or Attachments, update the paths used by templates and settings as well. Keeping those support paths at first makes it easier to learn the structure.
