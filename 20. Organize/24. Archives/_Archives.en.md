# Finish the work without losing what it taught you

Completed projects mixed with active work make it harder to see what needs attention. Yet the research, templates, and hard-won understanding may be useful again.

*Archives* lets you take those records out of the active workspace while keeping a route back to them.

## Leave an account of what happened

Before archiving a four-week reading experiment, write a short account of the result. What helped? What would you change if you did it again? What is still unresolved?

A few months later, you can begin a related project with what you learned instead of rereading every entry.

`📦Closed-Projects` holds finished projects, including those you chose to stop. For a stopped project, record why you stopped and what would make it worth restarting.

## Redistribute reusable material before archiving

Review what the project contains. Move generally useful knowledge, methods, templates, and references to an appropriate place in Resources. Keep the project’s own plans, progress, results, and reflection together as its record.

For the reading experiment, put an improved note template in Resources for future reading. Preserve the account of the four-week trial here. Link to the moved material from the completed project so its results still make sense.

**Make the reusable material easy to find while preserving the context of the finished work.** See [[_Resources.en]] for that side of the cycle.

## Age alone does not make a record inactive

An old project can still be active if you are responsible for it. Recent material can be archived if it no longer has an active role. Judge by its current use.

In *PARA*, Archives can also hold areas you no longer maintain and resources outside your current interests. They remain available if their role changes again.

When you begin something new in [[_Projects.en]], look here too. An earlier project may already contain a useful starting point.
