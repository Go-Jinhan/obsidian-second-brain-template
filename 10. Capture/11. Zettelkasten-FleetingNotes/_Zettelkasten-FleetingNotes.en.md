# You can write a thought before you can explain it

“These two things seem related.”

“Why do I get stuck here every time?”

“Would this be easier if I changed something?”

Thoughts often arrive as questions, impressions, or faint connections. A *Fleeting Note* catches one of them. It only needs enough detail for you to pick up the thinking later.

## Leave a clue for your future self

“Improve reading notes” may mean little a few days from now. A little context makes it useful:

> I highlighted a lot while reading, but after closing the book I did not know what I wanted to say about it.
>
> What if I tried explaining the most important point in one sentence after reading?

It does not have to be a major insight. If you can remember what prompted it and what you were wondering, you have somewhere to begin again. Speed can matter more than polished sentences here.

## Writing it down gives you something to think with

When you return to the note, you might look for reading methods or try the idea yourself. Put what you learn from a source in a *Reference Note* and connect it to the question. Create a project if you want to run an experiment.

Your question may change: perhaps the difficulty comes from reading too much at once rather than from the note-taking method. Record that change. The note gives your thinking a place to develop.

If you arrive at an explanation that stands on its own, develop it in [[_Zettelkasten-PermanentNotes.en]]. If it is a possibility you want to keep considering but not act on yet, use [[_Ideas.en]].

Start with one passing thought and one sentence about the moment it occurred to you.
