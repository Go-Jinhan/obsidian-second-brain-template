# Give yourself something useful to come back to

You read something worth keeping and save it. But when you sit down to write or begin a task, you cannot remember where it went. When you finally find it, it is too long to read again. You end up repeating the search.

This vault helps reduce that repetition. Notes hold the material so you can spend more of your attention thinking, connecting, and doing the work.

> [!tip] A question to keep in mind
> What would make this record useful when I return to it later?

That is a useful way to judge the structure too: can you understand a note and use it again?

## From a passing thought to a next action

While reading, you wonder why you understand a passage now but struggle to explain it later. Put the question in *Fleeting Notes* under *Capture*. A polished title can wait.

Later, you find a relevant article. In *Reference Notes*, record its source and your understanding. Add a short summary at the beginning so you can recognize the material without rereading the whole thing. This is *Distill*.

To test the idea, start a four-week reading experiment. Gather the relevant notes and references into the project folder and use them together. As you learn, explain an idea in your own words in a *Permanent Note* if it could be useful beyond this project.

At the end, return reusable reading methods and templates to *Resources*. Keep the experiment’s progress and results in *Archives*. What you learned can then support another project or piece of writing.

## Why bring several methods together?

*CODE* describes the flow: Capture, Organize, Distill, Express. *PARA* helps you choose where information will be useful: Projects, Areas, Resources, Archives. *Zettelkasten* helps you develop your understanding and connect ideas.

Subject folders help you find material, summaries help you read it, and links lead to related thoughts. These functions do not need four matching CODE folders. Here, Distill stays inside each note, with the details it summarizes.

The top-level structure has three places: **10. Capture catches material; 20. Organize gives it a useful home; 30. Express holds ideas explained in your own words.** Creating an actual result also happens in project folders or other tools.

## How much do you need to use at first?

Write one thought or source using [[_Capture.en]], then open it again a few days later. Can you tell what it means, why you kept it, and where you might use it? That is a useful beginning.

If you are unsure where it belongs, read [[_Organize.en]]. For summaries, read [[_Distill.en]]. To put something into practice, read [[_Projects.en]]. There is no need to fill every folder or turn every reference into a permanent note.

Notes beginning with `_` are guides. Open the one in a folder when you need help. [[_Second-Brain-Design.en]] explains how the pieces fit together and lists the references behind them.
