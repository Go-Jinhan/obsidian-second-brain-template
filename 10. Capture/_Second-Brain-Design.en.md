# Why is the vault arranged this way?

Finding a saved source is only helpful if you can understand it again. Understanding it also needs a route into something you want to do.

This vault connects **capturing, finding, understanding, connecting, and using**. Each folder and template helps with a different part of that process.

## Consider both the flow and the place

*CODE* describes capturing information, organizing it, distilling its essentials, and expressing what you know. *PARA* chooses a place according to the material’s use.

*Projects* holds work you want to finish; *Areas* holds responsibilities you maintain; *Resources* holds subject-based references; *Archives* holds inactive records. The question is when and why you would look for the material again.

*Capture* gives thoughts and sources an entry point before their place is clear. You can decide where they will be useful when you review them.

## Projects gather materials and return what is reusable

At the beginning of a project, bring relevant material from across the vault into its folder. Working materials and progress records stay close enough to use together.

At the end, return reusable knowledge and references to Resources. Keep the completed project’s context and results in Archives. The next project can then begin with materials you have already developed. [[_Projects.en]] explains this cycle in detail.

## Keep a summary with the note it describes

*Distill* extracts a note’s essentials. Putting it at the top lets you read the main point first and continue into details and sources when necessary.

When the material moves, the summary and its basis stay together. The same note supports both a quick visit and a closer reading. That is why CODE’s four steps do not require four separate folders here. See [[_Distill.en]] for the writing practice.

## Help a project begin and resume

*Twyla Tharp First Memo* records why you are beginning and what you want to achieve. *Project Progress* collects work, decisions, materials, and smaller results. *The Hemingway Bridge* records where you stopped and what to do next.

Together, they preserve direction, current state, and a way back into the work after an interruption.

*Intermediate Packets* encourage useful smaller pieces of work that can also be reused. An *Archipelago of Ideas* helps arrange existing materials into a result. *Reduce the Scope* helps choose a part you can finish now. [[_Projects.en]] shows how these approaches fit the project workflow.

## Classification and connections serve different needs

Resource folders make a subject easy to browse. *Wikilinks* let you follow relationships across locations. *Metadata* and tags help you find notes by a property such as type or topic.

The *Zettelkasten* distinction between Fleeting, Reference, and Permanent Notes helps with passing thoughts, sourced material, and independently explained ideas. A permanent note develops one idea in your own words and connects its reasoning so it can be used elsewhere.

Notes in the same folder need not make the same argument, and related ideas can live in different folders. See [[_Resources.en]] for organization and [[_Zettelkasten-PermanentNotes.en]] for developing ideas.

## Give yourself time to return

Daily notes keep experiences; weekly notes help identify patterns and adjustments; monthly notes help review goals and ongoing responsibilities.

During these reviews, return to Capture, develop useful thoughts, and choose a next action. [[_Periodic-Notes.en]] gives the practice a recurring place in your routine.

## Adapt the structure around its purpose

The subjects and numbers in Resources are examples. Change them as needed. If you move Books, Periodic Notes, Templates, or Attachments, also update the settings and template paths that use those locations.

The useful question is whether you can understand and reuse a record later. Begin with [[_Getting-Started.en]] and put one note to work.

## References

- Tiago Forte, *Building a Second Brain*; Korean translation by Seo Eun-kyung, 2023. The references in this vault draw on CODE, PARA, Progressive Summarization, creative execution, and review practices.
- Sönke Ahrens, *How to Take Smart Notes*, published in Korean as *제텔카스텐*. A reference for writing and connecting ideas.
- Kim Ik-han, *거인의 노트*, a Korean book on recording and reflection. Relevant to revisiting notes and developing the thinking in them.
- Command Space / CMDS knowledge-management materials. References for classification, indexes, and connections.

The numbering, subject names, and detailed forms in this vault are adaptations. They do not reproduce an official classification scheme or template from these sources. Situations described in the guides are examples of how to use the vault.
